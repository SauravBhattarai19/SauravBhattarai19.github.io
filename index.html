01 · Research

Models and open data fail in mirror image.

Physics-based hydrological models carry mechanism but lack coverage. Open Earth data carry coverage but lack mechanism. Each is the other's missing half, yet they are rarely joined, and the reason is easy to miss: calibration. A model tuned to observed discharge absorbs the errors of its inputs in its parameters, so it cannot tell good data from bad, and it needs a discharge record that the most flood-exposed places do not have.

My dissertation builds the chain that joins them. Open data are made accessible, refined to the local scale, and combined into records of interacting hazards. They feed an open, modular model with few parameters, each starting from open data or a published equation. With little to tune, errors in the data surface in the hydrograph instead of hiding in the parameters, and the model becomes an instrument for judging the data that drive it.

02 · Open tools

Tools anyone can run.

Open science is the thread through my work. The tools are free, documented, and already used in classrooms and workshops.

Python package · MIT license · v0.3.0 on PyPI

MRRpy

Rainfall–runoff and flood routing for any basin on Earth, from a DEM and a storm.

  • Runoff physics you can switch: Green–Ampt infiltration with soil recovery, variable source area, impervious surfaces, or SCS curve number, combined without counting rain twice.
  • Routing derived from the Saint-Venant equations: kinematic, Muskingum–Cunge, semi-implicit diffusive, and local-inertial schemes.
  • An exact, linear-time implicit solver on the drainage tree; water conserved to a median of 3 parts per million.
  • Python, a command-line wizard, a Jupyter form, and a QGIS plugin; runs on CPU, or on GPU 34 times faster at 1.38 million cells.

GeoClimate Intelligence Platform

Earth Engine's catalog, without writing a line of code.

  • 1,042 datasets and 23 climate indices across nine tools, from data download, climate indices, and return periods to raster math and multi-geometry export.
  • No install and no code: a 30-year daily rainfall analysis for a 784 km² area runs in under a minute.
  • Earth Engine implementations of two ERDC algorithms: GLOBAL-SAM snow depth and SERVES root-zone soil moisture.
  • Used by 16 undergraduates in Advanced Water Resources Engineering (Fall 2025) and in a faculty workshop with 28 registered faculty.
  • Best in Show and first place, Microsoft × AUC Data Science Initiative Data Challenge (2025).

05 · About

Civil engineer turned hydrologist.

I trained as a civil engineer at Pulchowk Campus in Nepal and came to Jackson State University in 2023 to work with Prof. Rocky Talchabhadel. From 2024 to 2026 I was also an ORISE research fellow at the U.S. Army Engineer Research and Development Center's Coastal and Hydraulics Laboratory, working with Dr. Nawa Raj Pradhan. My work moves between two very different places: the data-rich Mississippi River Basin and the data-sparse Nepal Himalaya, where the same open data have to do very different jobs.

Before flood modeling I worked on heat: my M.S. thesis followed heatwaves from risk assessment to nature-based cooling and produced four of my papers. Teaching is part of the same project. I have built course platforms, taught Python and Earth Engine workshops, and mentored undergraduates at three universities.
